# Monopoly lens

Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Small, definable starting market: French architecture practices, with French-law specifics (loi MOP, ACT/DET/AOR, CCTP/DPGF, Factur-X, PLU/cadastre). A narrow beachhead that generic project tools do not serve natively.
- The secret is plausible: the builder is a practising architect who uses the product in his own agency, so he knows workflows outsiders do not (fee-note splits between co-contractors, DET/OPR reserves, tender-company tracking).
- Depth is real: the product covers the whole project lifecycle in one place, which creates switching cost once an agency's projects, documents, price library and history live inside it.
- Compounding data asset: the firm's own price library and observed prices from tenders accumulate with use and are costly to rebuild elsewhere.
- Timing argument exists: French e-invoicing obligations (Factur-X / EN 16931) force agencies to change tools. Whether the dates and scope really apply to this buyer is unverified (see questions).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No distribution.** The brief says acquisition is undefined. A deep product with no known path to the first 100 agencies is not yet a business, and the buyer pool is a few thousand-seat-sized offices reached through word of mouth, professional bodies and events.
2. **Being "better" is not 10x, and breadth is easy to copy.** Features are broad but each module competes with a specialist (accounting, planning, document tools, French architecture software). Nothing in the brief shows one thing that is ten times better than the next option, or a network effect, scale advantage or brand. A well-funded incumbent adding modules could close the gap.
3. **A tiny, price-capped market.** At 29 and 59 EUR a month with small teams, revenue per customer is low, so the total reachable revenue may be too small to support a company, and one maintainer's time is the limit on durability (ten-year hold, support, regulatory updates).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else sells to the same architect today, and what do your first ten users use instead? (open: /founder-competitors)
- How many French architecture agencies are there by size, and what share is reachable? Not asserted here. (open: /founder-competitors, /founder-consumer)
- How will the first 100 agencies arrive, and what does each cost to win? (open: /founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- What is the single thing you do ten times better than the alternatives, in a way an architect would switch for?
- What stops a copycat in month six, once a competitor reads your feature list?
- What do you know from practice that a competitor would not guess, and is it built in or just described?
- Which e-invoicing deadlines apply to your buyers, and is that a real forcing event? (open: /founder-competitors for the market, a legal check for the dates)
- At 29 to 59 EUR, how many paying agencies cover your costs, including AI usage and hosting? (open: /founder-cfo)
- How much of the product do users actually use, and which module do they would not give up?
- Who maintains this in year five if you are busy running your agency?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name the one wedge module (the one thing ten times better than current options) and sell that first, with evidence from real users that they would switch for it.
2. Show a concrete channel that produced paying agencies beyond the founder's own network (for example 10 paying agencies not known to him), with the measured cost per win.
3. Complete the competitor map and the unit economics (/founder-competitors, /founder-cfo) and show a path to a revenue level that supports the business at the current price points.
4. Name a lock-in that grows with use (price library, project history, shared data between agencies and companies) and show that it is being built on purpose.
