# Offers lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Pain is real and specific.** French architecture offices must run MOP phases, DPGF/CCTP, Factur-X invoicing and site reports. Today that is usually several disconnected tools plus spreadsheets. The product covers the whole lifecycle in one place, in French, with French obligations built in.
- **Likelihood of the outcome is credible.** The product already exists and is used daily by a practising architect in his own agency. That is proof of use, though not yet proof of purchase by others.
- **Clean price ladder.** Free trial, Starter 29 EUR, Pro 59 EUR, Enterprise on quote. A low-risk entry and a natural upgrade path. The AI is gated to the top tier, which gives a reason to move up.
- **Effort is partly removed for the buyer.** Offline desktop client, PWA, autosave, automatic document numbering and Factur-X compliance reduce admin work the buyer would otherwise do by hand.
- **Domain depth is hard to fake.** Detail such as fee breakdowns across co-contractors, certificats de paiement and ACT consultation workflows shows a builder who knows the trade.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The offer is a feature list, not a stack around a dream outcome.** The brief says what the software covers, not what result the architect gets (for example: invoiced faster, fewer unpaid fees, fewer hours of admin per project). With no named outcome, no time-to-result and no guarantee, the buyer compares it to cheaper or familiar tools, and the offer becomes a commodity priced on subscription cost.
2. **Switching effort is very high and nothing offsets it.** Moving live projects, price libraries, contacts and numbering into a new system is the largest sacrifice the buyer makes. The brief mentions no migration help, onboarding, or risk reversal. Likelihood and effort are the two weakest terms of the value equation here.
3. **Reach and market size are unproven.** Acquisition is "not defined". Nothing in the brief shows the buyers can be found cheaply, or that enough of them will pay. The 29 to 59 EUR price points signal a volume game, which only works if reach is cheap, and the AI cost per token can erode margin at the top tier.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single outcome does a paying agency buy this for, and in what time frame do they see it?
- Who besides your own agency uses it today, and are any of them paying? What do they say it replaced?
- What do architects use now, and what makes them stop? What is the one reason to leave their current tool?
- What does moving an existing agency over involve, in hours and risk? Who does the work?
- What is the guarantee if the migration or the first month fails?
- Could you double the price for a defined segment (for example 5 to 10 person offices) and still be the obvious choice?
- How will the first 100 agencies learn about this, and what does each cost?

Open questions I cannot answer from the brief (nothing here is asserted as fact):
- Number of French architecture agencies and share that would buy software: /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer.
- Which tools they use now, their prices and public complaints: /founder-competitors.
- Willingness to pay at 29 / 59 EUR and the price that fits the segment: /founder-consumer, then /founder-pricing.
- Cost to acquire a customer, churn, AI cost per customer, margin by tier, and what a guarantee would cost: /founder-cfo.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Write the offer around one measurable outcome and a time frame, and name it, instead of a module list.
2. Add a migration-and-onboarding component (import of existing projects and contacts, a guided first project) and a risk-reversal guarantee the CFO confirms is affordable.
3. Show at least a handful of non-founder agencies paying, or a real pre-sale, before further build.
4. Define one acquisition channel with a tested cost per customer, and confirm margin per tier including AI cost.
