# Product lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Deep domain fit: the product speaks the real vocabulary and obligations of French architecture (loi MOP phases, CCTP/DPGF, ACT/DET/AOR, Factur-X). It was built by a practising architect who uses it in his own agency, so the workflows come from real use, not guesses.
- It owns a wide slice of the experience: proposal to invoice to site report to as-built file in one place. Fewer seams between tools is the right instinct.
- Craft shows in the parts nobody sees: offline mode, idempotent invoice numbering, a single document letterhead across PDF, Word and Excel, mobile-first site reserves.
- Pricing tiers are simple to read (29 / 59 EUR, then quote).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** The brief lists proposals, tenders, invoices, specs, Gantt, site reports, cadastre, PLU, AI agents, mail, calendar and accounting sync. The Product lens cannot state the one thing this does brilliantly. A buyer comparing it to a tool they already use sees a long feature list, not a reason to switch. The one-sentence pitch does not exist yet.
2. **The first five minutes are unproven.** Nothing in the brief says what a new architect sees on day one, how long until the first real value, or how existing projects, contacts and documents get in. A suite this broad is heavy to adopt, and an empty tool is a dead tool.
3. **Seams and trust in the money and legal parts.** Invoicing, numbering and accounting connectors (Zoho, Odoo, Chorus Pro, SuperPDP) are where an error costs the customer real money and legal exposure. Every handoff between systems is a place where the experience can break, and one bad invoice ends trust. Related: a single founder-architect building and supporting all of it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Say what this is in one sentence, for one type of agency. Which module would you keep if you had to delete all the others?
- Describe the first five minutes of a new agency: what do they do, and what do they see?
- How does an agency with years of existing projects and invoices move in? How long does that take?
- Which three features do users open every day, and which ones are barely touched? (Open question: no usage data in the brief.)
- Which tools does an agency use today that this must replace, and what makes them switch?
- Where is the experience handed between people or systems (mail, accounting connector, e-invoicing platform), and what happens when it fails?
- Who answers support when an invoice or a legal document goes wrong?
- Open questions I cannot answer from the brief: market size of French architecture agencies (/founder-consumer), who the direct competitors are and what they charge (/founder-competitors), margin on the 29 EUR plan after hosting and AI costs (/founder-cfo).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Write the one-sentence pitch and the one job the product does best, and reorder the home screen and the onboarding around it.
- Show a measured onboarding: time from signup to first real value for a new agency, with a working import path for existing data.
- Show usage evidence from real agencies other than the founder's own (even five), and cut or hide modules nobody uses.
- Put the invoicing and e-invoicing path under visible quality control (tests, a clear failure message, a support route) before selling it as the core.
- Validate competitors, willingness to pay and unit economics through /founder-competitors, /founder-consumer and /founder-cfo before any scaling spend.
